Threshold Discount or Percentage Off First? Check the Threshold, Then the Amount You Actually Pay

The same '20% off (pay 80%)' and '30 off when you spend 200' can end up tens of yuan apart at checkout. The difference is not in the multiplication but in the rules: what amount the threshold is judged on, whether the percentage or the amount comes off first, and whether shipping counts. This guide takes two complete orders apart under three different rule sets, step by step, so you can re-check every line yourself.

Written by Ernzi; worked examples re-checked · Updated: 2026-11-04

The figures in this article are teaching examples constructed by this site and do not represent any merchant's real promotion. Platform rules change; when you order, the breakdown on the checkout page governs. The value of the examples is the method — substitute your own numbers.

First, break the promotion into three questions

When you see a coupon or a block of promotion terms, do not start calculating. Write down three things first:

  1. What amount is the threshold judged on: the original-price total of the items, the amount after the percentage discount, or the amount actually paid?
  2. What is the order: percentage off first, then the amount off — or the amount off first, then the percentage? Is there a second layer (such as an extra member discount on top)?
  3. Which amounts participate: do all items take part? Does shipping count toward the discount? Which amount is the free-shipping threshold judged on?

If any one of these differs, the result differs. Most people get it wrong not because they cannot multiply, but because they assume a rule set the merchant is not using. The two orders below run through the three most common rule sets.

Order one: 240 yuan, 20% off, 30 off over 200

Assume no shipping and that all items participate. The three rule sets give:

Rule setCalculationAmount paid
Threshold on original price, percentage off first, then amount off240 ≥ 200, coupon usable; 240 × 0.8 − 30162 yuan
Threshold on original price, amount off first, then percentage off240 ≥ 200, coupon usable; (240 − 30) × 0.8168 yuan
Threshold on the discounted amount240 × 0.8 = 192 < 200, coupon cannot be used192 yuan

The first two differ by only 6 yuan, and the source is clear: when the amount comes off first, that 30 yuan is itself discounted to 80%, so it actually saves only 24 yuan. The third differs from the first by a full 30 yuan — not a miscalculation, but the coupon never took effect at all. When most people cannot reconcile a bill, this is where it went wrong: they calculated as if the coupon had applied, while the merchant charged on the basis that the discounted amount missed the threshold.

A simple principle for judging order: when the threshold can be met either way, percentage off first and amount off second is better for the buyer; but if applying the percentage first drops the amount below the threshold, it can void the whole coupon instead. In that case compare ‘what you pay with the coupon’ against ‘skipping the percentage discount and meeting the threshold directly’ — do not stack every offer unconditionally.

Order two: 359 yuan, 15% off, 50 off over 300, plus shipping

Real orders usually add a shipping layer. Assume: original price 359 yuan, a storewide 15% off (pay 85%), 50 off over 300; shipping is 8 yuan, and orders actually paid at 288 yuan or more ship free (judged on the final amount paid). The three rule sets:

Rule setCalculationPaid incl. shipping
Threshold on original price, percentage off first, then amount off359 ≥ 300; 359 × 0.85 − 50 = 255.15, below 288, add 8 shipping263.15 yuan
Threshold on original price, amount off first, then percentage off(359 − 50) × 0.85 = 262.65, below 288, add 8 shipping270.65 yuan
Threshold judged after discount (threshold 320 yuan)359 × 0.85 = 305.15 < 320, coupon cannot be used; 305.15 already reaches 288, free shipping305.15 yuan

This example adds an easily missed chain reaction: the harder you use the offers, the lower the amount paid, and the more likely you are to fall out of the free-shipping band. The first rule set gives the lowest item price, yet because it lands 32.85 yuan short of the free-shipping threshold, you pay 8 yuan of shipping — the total is still the lowest here, but if the shortfall were only a yuan or two, adding one small item could actually save money, which is exactly why padding an order for free shipping deserves its own calculation.

When comparing two stores there is one more principle: compare the final amount paid for the same items in the same quantities. One store may count shipping toward its threshold while the other does not; calculate each under its own rules, then compare the totals including shipping. Comparing discount strength alone (10% off versus 15% off) is meaningless — thresholds and shipping often eat the difference back. Two small traps: stacked percentages and rounding

Discount on discount multiplies; it does not add. 20% off followed by 10% off is 0.8 × 0.9 = 0.72, i.e. you pay 72%; a 100 yuan item ends up at 72 yuan. Mentally adding ‘minus 20%, then minus 10%’ into ‘30% off’ undercounts by 2 yuan, and the gap grows with the amount.

Differences of a few cents are usually rounding. Platforms commonly round each item’s discounted price to the cent, item by item, then sum and apply coupons; this site’s tools calculate on the whole order and round once at the end. A multi-item order differing by a few cents is a normal rule-set difference and not worth agonizing over; a difference of several yuan or more means going back to the three questions above and checking them one by one.

Pre-checkout checklist

  1. Is the coupon threshold judged on the original price, the discounted amount, or the amount paid? On the checkout page, is the coupon lit up or greyed out?
  2. Is the order percentage-off-first or amount-off-first? Can member discounts and category coupons stack, and do the terms say ‘cannot be combined’?
  3. Are any items excluded (pre-sales, flash sales, specified categories)? Does the amount of excluded items count toward the threshold?
  4. How much is shipping? Which amount is the free-shipping threshold judged on? After using the offers, are you still over the free-shipping line?
  5. Have you counted points, cashback or free gifts into your ‘amount saved’? They are not part of what you pay today.
  6. Final step: pay based on the breakdown amounts on the checkout page, not the headline numbers on the promotion page.

FAQ

Is the threshold judged on the original price or the discounted price?

Both exist; it depends on the merchant’s rules. Terms saying ‘orders over X yuan’ usually mean the combined amount of the items participating in the promotion; ‘actual payment over X yuan’ means the amount after discounts. Before ordering, take the checkout page as the authority on whether the offer lights up — the third rule set in this article is exactly the case where the discounted amount misses the threshold and the coupon never takes effect.

Which is better value: percentage off first then threshold discount, or the reverse?

When the threshold is met either way, percentage off first is better value, because the threshold amount is then deducted in full from the discounted price; taking the amount off first effectively discounts that amount too. In the 240 yuan example the two orders differ by 6 yuan — precisely 30 yuan times the undiscounted 20%.

Does shipping participate in threshold discounts and percentage discounts?

Generally not. Shipping is usually added after the discounts are calculated, and free shipping is then judged by the merchant’s rules. When comparing two stores, include shipping in the final amount paid before comparing; comparing item prices alone leads to the wrong conclusion.

Is 20% off followed by 10% off the same as 30% off?

No. Successive discounts multiply: 0.8 × 0.9 = 0.72, i.e. you pay 72%. Adding the two percentages directly (20% + 10% = 30%) is a common mistake.

Why does the calculator’s result differ from the checkout page by a few cents?

Usually a different rounding method: the platform may round each item to the cent before summing, while this site’s tools round once at the end on the whole order. A few cents is a rule-set difference; the checkout page breakdown governs in the end.

Should points, cashback and free gifts count as part of the discount?

Do not count them into what you pay today. Points and cashback are future, conditional benefits, and the value of a free gift varies by person. When comparing offers, compare only the cash actually paid today, and record points and cashback as a separate line.

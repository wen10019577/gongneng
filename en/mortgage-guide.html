Equal Payment vs Equal Principal: What's the Real Difference? A 1-Million-Yuan, 20-Year Loan, Worked Out

The line you hear most often when choosing a repayment method is 'equal principal saves more interest, equal payment is easier on the budget'. How much it saves and how big the pressure difference is — few people work out the actual numbers. This guide spreads the two methods out item by item on the same loan: monthly payment, total interest, how much principal is repaid in the first few years — all re-checkable.

Written by Ernzi; worked examples re-checked · Updated: 2026-11-04

This guide is a teaching calculation: a loan of 1,000,000 yuan over 20 years (240 periods) at a fixed assumed annual rate of 3.6%, which does not represent any bank's actual pricing. Your contracted rate, the interest convention from the disbursement date, LPR movements and prepayment terms follow your loan contract; with a different rate the numbers change, but the comparison method in this guide does not.

The essential difference between the two methods, in one sentence

Equal payment: the monthly repayment amount is fixed. Each month’s interest is calculated on the remaining principal, and the rest of the payment repays principal. Early on the balance is high and interest heavy, so principal is repaid slowly; later it reverses.

Equal principal: the principal repaid each month is fixed (total ÷ number of periods), and interest is calculated separately on the remaining principal, so the monthly payment decreases month by month. The monthly payment is higher early on, but the principal falls fast.

Interest is always calculated only on how much principal you still owe that month — not a cent more. The two methods’ total interest differs for exactly one reason: how fast the principal falls. Whoever brings the balance down faster pays less total interest. Once you understand that, none of the numbers below needs memorising.

A few details worth noting:

  • The first month’s payments differ by 1315.56 yuan. Equal principal’s first month is 7166.67 yuan (4166.67 principal + 3000 interest), over 20% more than equal payment. That is exactly its entry barrier: not that the overall deal is bad, but that the cash flow of the first two or three years has to withstand it.
  • The two payment lines cross at around year 9. Equal principal’s payment falls by about 12.50 yuan a month, and needs roughly 105 months to come down from 7166.67 yuan to level with 5851.11 yuan; after the crossing, equal principal’s payment drops below equal payment’s, and the gap keeps widening.
  • The principal repaid in the first year differs by over 15,000 yuan. Under equal payment, about 70213 yuan is repaid in the first year in total, of which interest accounts for 35429.80 yuan and principal for only 34783.58 yuan; under equal principal, a full 50000 yuan of principal is repaid in the first year. This explains why, for people planning to sell the home or settle the loan within three to five years, the real gap between the two methods is even bigger than the total-interest difference suggests — the outstanding balances differ.

How to choose: by your cash flow and holding period, not by total interest

Cases that favour equal principal: stable income, with the early monthly payment not a high share of income (a suggestion: the first month’s payment should not exceed a third to 40% of household monthly income); you plan to hold long-term and aren’t in a hurry to move; and you have no higher-yielding, low-risk use for the money. The 40,000-plus yuan of interest saved is real.

Cases that favour equal payment: cash flow is tight after the down payment, with major expenses such as renovation or children in the first years; income is expected to rise (low pressure early, and as income grows the payment share falls naturally); or you plan to settle early within a few years — in that case the two methods’ early balances differ by only about 15,000 yuan, and the interest gap is nowhere near 42,700 yuan, so don’t strain yourself over the total-interest figure.

The most common wrong choice: forcing equal principal ‘to save interest’, ending up with early payments so heavy that daily cash flow seizes up and you’re driven to high-interest consumer loans or credit-card instalments — the mortgage interest saved is nowhere near enough to pay the interest over there. The first principle of a repayment method is always that the monthly payment is sustainable; total interest comes second.

Two judgements about early repayment

What you prepay is always principal. There is no such thing as ‘the first years are all interest, so prepaying isn’t worth it’: each period’s interest is settled that period on the balance, and the moment you prepay a sum of principal at any point, every later period’s interest is calculated on the lower balance. The idea that ‘after five years of paying, the interest is all paid’ is a misunderstanding — the interest paid corresponds to the principal genuinely occupied during those five years.

Shortening the term saves more interest than reducing the monthly payment. After a prepayment you choose one of two: keep the payment the same and shorten the term, which saves more total interest; or keep the term and reduce the payment, which wins on easier cash flow. If the goal is clearly saving interest, choose shortening the term; if the goal is easing pressure, choose reducing the payment. Before you act, check the contract: the minimum prepayment amount, how many times a year, and whether a penalty applies vary greatly between banks. FAQ

Which has less total interest: equal payment or equal principal?

With the same loan amount, rate and term and no early repayment, equal principal has less total interest. In this guide’s example, equal principal’s total interest is 361500 yuan and equal payment’s is 404268 yuan, a difference of 42768 yuan. The reason: equal principal repays more principal early on, the outstanding balance falls faster, and interest is calculated on the balance, so it’s naturally less.

Why is most of the early monthly payment interest under equal payment?

Interest is calculated on the outstanding balance for the month. Right after the loan is drawn, the balance is at its highest (1,000,000 yuan), so the first month’s interest alone is 3000 yuan; the equal-payment monthly amount is fixed at 5851 yuan, so in the early payments interest makes up more than half. As the balance falls, interest decreases month by month and the principal share slowly rises.

I’ve already chosen equal payment — can I switch to equal principal midway?

Most banks don’t support switching between repayment methods, or require a fresh review and charges; the specifics follow your loan contract and the handling bank’s policy. A more workable alternative is to keep equal payment and prepay part of the principal alongside it, which approximates the extra early principal of equal principal — but first confirm the contract’s terms on prepayment.

Does a prepayment repay interest first or principal first?

A prepayment repays principal. Each period’s interest is in any case settled month by month on the remaining principal — there is no unpaid interest waiting for you to clear first. After you prepay a sum of principal, subsequent interest is calculated on the lower balance. Choosing to shorten the term saves more total interest than reducing the monthly payment.

Does this comparison table still hold when rates float?

The qualitative conclusion holds; the numbers change. After a floating-rate loan is repriced each year, the monthly payment is recalculated on the new rate and the remaining principal, and the total interest of both methods changes with it. This guide uses a fixed assumed rate so the difference between the two methods can be isolated for comparison; when you sign, recalculate with the actual rate your bank offers.

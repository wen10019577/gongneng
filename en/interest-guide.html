How Do You Compare Deposit Interest? 100,000 Yuan for Three Years: Compound Interest Didn't Beat a Higher Simple Rate

When comparing deposits, many people first ask 'is it simple or compound interest?' — but that's the wrong order of questions. This guide works it out on 100,000 yuan: a one-year deposit at 1.5%, rolled over with compounding for three years, earns 4567.84 yuan of interest; a straight three-year deposit at 1.9% simple interest earns 5700 yuan. A 0.4-percentage-point rate difference crushes the compounding effect with ease.

Written by Ernzi; worked examples re-checked · Updated: 2026-11-04

The rates in this guide are assumed figures for demonstration and do not represent any bank's posted prices. Deposit rates change frequently — when you act, go by the bank's posted rate that day and the product terms; large-denomination certificates of deposit, treasury bonds and the like have their own rules and are outside this guide's scope.

First, tell them apart: simple vs compound — the difference is whether interest earns interest

Simple interest: interest is calculated only on the original principal — interest = principal × annual rate × years. Ordinary time deposits are simple-interest within their term. Compound interest: each period’s interest is added to the principal, and the next period earns interest on the interest too. Compounding in deposits only happens across periods: on automatic renewal at maturity, or when a one-year deposit is redeposited with principal and interest after a year. Work out the magnitude first, then talk about whether it matters.

Main example: 100,000 yuan, two outcomes after three years

MethodCalculationPrincipal + interest after three yearsTotal interest
One-year at 1.5%, redeposited with principal and interest at maturity (compound)100000 × 1.015³104567.84 yuan4567.84 yuan
Three-year at 1.9%, deposited for the full term (simple)100000 × (1 + 1.9% × 3)105700.00 yuan5700.00 yuan

A difference of 1132.16 yuan. How much does the compound route’s ‘interest on interest’ actually roll out over three years? If 1.5% were held at simple interest for three years, the interest would be 4500 yuan, so compounding gives only 67.84 yuan extra. The difference between a 1.5% and a 1.9% rate, by contrast, is worth on the order of 1200 yuan. Conclusion: when comparing deposits, compare the same-term rate first, then how well the term matches your needs, and the interest-payment method last. And how much does the advertised ‘monthly interest payment’ differ? Worked out for you

Some products emphasise monthly interest settlement and compounding rolls. Take 10,000 yuan at the same nominal annual rate of 3.6% for one year: settled yearly, you get 10360.00 yuan; settled monthly (monthly rate 0.3%), you get 10000 × (1 + 0.3%)¹² = 10366.00 yuan. A difference of 6 yuan over the year; even scaled to 100,000 yuan of principal it’s only 60 yuan. The real value of monthly interest payment is cash flow: a sum of interest arriving every month suits topping up daily expenses — not higher returns. Don’t accept a lower stated rate for the sake of the word ‘compound’.

Before comparing sizes, dodge two interest traps

Early withdrawal is counted at the demand rate. A three-year time deposit taken out after two and a half years because you need the money: the withdrawn part generally earns interest at the demand deposit rate on the withdrawal day (on the order of 0.05%–0.1%) — the two and a half years of time-deposit interest basically zeroes out, and can end up less than honestly rolling a one-year deposit would have given. Before depositing long-term, set aside emergency funds first — the same reasoning as keeping a safety cushion when prepaying a mortgage.

Automatic renewal uses the new rate. Renewal uses the posted rate on the renewal day, not the original contract rate. When rates are falling, the 1.9% you thought you had may quietly become 1.6%. Checking the posted rate in the bank’s app around the maturity date is a two-minute job.

One more, more common waste: idle money sitting in a demand account long-term

A far bigger difference than the simple-vs-compound argument hides in ‘leave it in the demand account for now, sort it out later’. 100,000 yuan in a demand account for a year, at a demonstration rate of 0.05%, earns just 50 yuan of interest; even a plain one-year time deposit (at a 1.5% demonstration basis) earns 1500 yuan — a difference of 1450 yuan, more than twenty times the compounding effect discussed above. In practice you don’t need to empty the demand account: keep 1–3 months of expenses as an emergency reserve, and layer the rest by ‘how long until it’s definitely needed’ — money needed within three months into money-market-fund-type products or short-term deposits, money not needed for over a year can then be compared on time-deposit rates. After layering, each layer’s comparison returns to this guide’s main line: compare the rate first, then liquidity, and only lastly the interest-payment method. If a five-figure sum sits in your salary account’s demand balance all year round, do this step first — it’s worth more than studying any compounding trick.

FAQ

How big is the difference between simple and compound interest on deposits?

A time deposit is simple-interest within its term; compounding only happens across periods, such as on automatic renewal. Over two or three years at rates of a few percent, the extra from compounding is limited: 10,000 yuan at 3.6%, interest paid monthly for a year, exceeds interest paid yearly by 6 yuan.

When comparing deposits, what should I look at first?

First the annual rate for the same term, then the term and liquidity (the loss from early withdrawal being counted at the demand rate), and only lastly the interest-payment method. A 0.4-percentage-point rate difference on 100,000 yuan over three years is worth 1132 yuan — far beyond the compounding effect.

How is interest calculated if I withdraw a time deposit early?

Generally it’s counted at the demand deposit rate on the withdrawal day, and the original time-deposit rate is void. Set aside emergency funds before depositing long-term, and don’t touch time deposits unless necessary.

Is the rate on automatic renewal the original one?

No — it executes at the bank’s posted rate for the same term on the renewal day, and may go up or down. Check the posted rate once before maturity before deciding.

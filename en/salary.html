Why take-home pay can shrink in the later months

This model computes cumulative tax on cumulative income, then subtracts the tax already withheld in previous months. As taxable income builds up over the year it can move into a higher bracket, so you cannot divide the year's tax by 12 and claim every month's withholding is the same.

Enter social insurance amounts from your payslip

Regions, contribution bases and personal circumstances all affect social insurance and housing fund amounts, so this site no longer presets a single nationwide contribution rate. Enter the amount you personally bear here, not your employer's contributions. Special additional deductions only reduce taxable income; they are not deducted again from your take-home pay.

Model limits and basis

Assumes you start in January and draw a salary from the same employer for 12 consecutive months, with salary and deductions unchanged, and no bonuses, tax-exempt income or special withholding arrangements. Starting or leaving mid-year, working for more than one employer, or a first job can all change the picture. Tax rates follow the comprehensive income schedule appended to the Individual Income Tax Law, and the calculation follows the State Taxation Administration's explanation of the cumulative withholding method; this page was checked on 2026-09-16. For an annual summary comparison, use the annual tax tool.

A complete example you can check against a payslip

With a monthly gross salary of 20000 yuan, employee social insurance and housing fund of 2500 yuan, and special additional deductions of 2000 yuan, each month adds 10500 yuan of taxable income. For the first 3 months the cumulative amount stays within 36000 yuan, so tax is 315 yuan a month and take-home pay is 17185 yuan; in month 4 the cumulative amount reaches 42000 yuan and enters the 10% bracket, so that month's tax is 735 yuan; from month 5 onwards the monthly tax settles at 1050 yuan and take-home pay at 16450 yuan. Total income tax for the year is 10080 yuan. The month-by-month derivation is in "Why Does Take-Home Pay Start Shrinking Mid-Year?"

Frequently asked questions

Has my employer withheld too much when my take-home pay drops? Usually not. Under cumulative withholding, the rate steps up as cumulative income crosses brackets — low at the start of the year, higher after a mid-year step-up is the normal pattern. The way to check is to apply the rate table to your cumulative amount for the year, not to look at a single month.

What happens if I add special additional deductions mid-year? Once added, your cumulative deductions increase in one go, that month's withholding drops sharply or even to zero, and any over-withholding is refunded or topped up at the annual reconciliation. Nothing is lost.

Is a year-end bonus included here? No. This tool models a fixed monthly salary. Whether a year-end bonus is taxed separately or merged into comprehensive income has to be compared at the annual level with the annual tax tool.

Can I run this tool separately for two salaries? You cannot simply add the results. With two employers, each withholds separately and the two are merged at reconciliation, which can differ a lot from the single-employer scenario — rely on the reconciliation.

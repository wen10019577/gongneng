Percentages: Share, Part Amount and Growth Rate

Keep three common questions separate: B% of A, A's share of B, and the rate of change from A to B.

Updated, rules checked: 2026-09-16 · How calculations work

Check with an example

15% of 200 is 30. Dropping from 200 to 170 is a change of −15%; going back from 170 to 200 is an increase of about 17.65%, not 15%.

Find the right denominator first

For a share question, the denominator is the total B; for a growth-rate question, the denominator is the original value A. Putting the new value in the denominator gives a different answer. Growth-rate mode requires the original value to be greater than 0; growth from zero, or change from a negative base, needs to be described separately — this page does not force a percentage for those cases.

Percent vs. percentage points

If a rate rises from 10% to 12%, it has increased by 2 percentage points; relative to the original 10%, it has grown by 20%. These express different relationships. To work out the relative increase, use growth-rate mode with A=10, B=12.

Common mistakes

Raise a price by 20% and then cut it by 20%, and the result is 96% of the original price, because the base changed for the second calculation. Likewise, the average discount across several products cannot be computed by simply averaging the percentages when the amounts differ; the percentages must be weighted by amount.

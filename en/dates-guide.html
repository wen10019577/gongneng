How Many Nights, When Does the Probation Period End, How Old in Full Years: Do Dates Count Both Ends?

When a date calculation goes wrong, it is rarely the addition or subtraction that is wrong — it is almost always the convention: does this span of days include the first day and the last day? Hotels count nights, probation periods include the start, age depends on the birthday — four frequent scenarios each have their own way of counting, and this guide lays them out once, with aligned date examples.

Written by Ernzi; worked examples re-checked · Updated: 2026-11-04

All dates in this guide are Gregorian calendar dates. For legal conventions such as contract terms and warranty periods, the wording of the document itself and the period-calculation rules of the Civil Code govern: when a period is calculated in days, the starting day is not counted and counting begins from the following day — which is exactly the "plain interval" convention below. When signing a contract, first confirm which set of rules it uses.

Scenario 1: how many nights — count only the interval

Check in on July 3, check out on July 6: interval = 6 − 3 = 3 nights. The trip touches 4 calendar days — the 3rd, 4th, 5th and 6th — but the unit you pay for is the “night”: the night of the 3rd, the night of the 4th, the night of the 5th. The extra night people often pay for when booking usually comes from mistaking “4 calendar days” for “4 nights”. The same applies when cars and equipment are rented by the day: first confirm whether the merchant uses a 24-hour basis or a calendar-day basis — don’t assume.

Scenario 2: the Nth day — includes the start, excludes the end

You start work on July 1, with an agreed probation assessment period of 7 days: July 1 is day 1, and day 7 is July 7. If you count by plain interval (the 1st + 7 days = the 8th), you end up a day late. “Check in for 7 consecutive days” promotions and “7-day trial” memberships use the start-inclusive convention; but “within 7 days from the date of signing” in a contract usually starts counting from the following day under the legal rules — signing on July 1, the last day of a 7-day period is July 8. The two conventions look alike and differ by a day; when you see “the Nth day” or “within N days”, stop and confirm first.

Scenario 3: countdowns — just ask how many days are left

Today is November 4; how many days until December 25? November has 30 − 4 = 26 days left, plus 25 days of December: 51 days in total. A countdown uses the plain interval and doesn’t count today; but if the question becomes “including today, how many days’ worth do I need to prepare” (stocking up on medicine, planning shifts, say), you add 1 and get 52 days. The same span of days gives answers differing by one depending on how the question is asked — think first about whether you need the “distance apart” or the “coverage”.

Scenario 4: age and leap years — two hidden variables

Age in full years depends on whether the birthday has passed. Someone born on November 5, 2000 is 25 years old on November 4, 2026 — one day short of the birthday, they don’t count as 26. This same logic applies in school enrolment, insurance enrolment and retirement estimates; checking with the age tool is the steadiest way. It differs from how cumulative years of service are counted for annual leave — for that, see the annual leave guide.

A leap year gives February an extra day. From February 28 to March 1: in 2025 (a common year) the interval is 1 day; in 2024 (a leap year) it is 2 days, with a February 29 in between. When calculating a rental term, interest or a probation period across February, first confirm whether that year is a leap year (divisible by 4, and for century years divisible by 400); deposit interest calculated by the day is real money if you count one day too many — for the conventions, cross-check the deposit interest guide.

Scenario 5: working days are not calendar days

“Processed in 3 working days” and “processed in 3 days” can differ by a whole weekend. Example: an application submitted on Friday, November 6, 2026, counting 3 working days from the following day: the 1st is Monday the 9th, the 2nd is Tuesday the 10th, the 3rd is Wednesday the 11th, when it is completed; whereas 3 calendar days only gets you to November 9. Conversely, the “about 5 days for delivery” a courier quotes usually means calendar days, and it stretches further across Spring Festival and National Day. When you see “working days”, first cross the weekends and public holidays off the calendar and then count; when you see “days”, take calendar days by default unless the document defines it otherwise — when shipping insurance takes effect, when a warranty starts, how long a visa stay lasts: all the same question.

FAQ

Which days are a 3-night hotel stay?

Nights = check-out date − check-in date. Checking in on July 3 and out on July 6 is 3 nights, spanning 4 calendar days. Don’t mistake the number of calendar days for nights and book a day too many.

How do I count “the Nth day” for probation periods and shelf lives?

Everyday agreements mostly use the start-inclusive convention: day 1 is the starting day itself, so 7 days starting July 1 end on July 7. Legal periods usually start counting from the following day. Confirm the convention first, then count the days.

Why is age in full years sometimes two years less than nominal age?

Age in full years is calculated by whether this year’s birthday has passed — if it hasn’t, subtract 1. Nominal age (xusui) grows with the lunar new year. A difference of 1–2 years between the two is normal; official business uses age in full years (the ID-card convention).

Notes

This guide is a compilation of everyday conventions; the calculation of legal periods is governed by Articles 199 to 204 of the Civil Code and the terms of the specific document.

Simple vs. Compound Interest: How Much Difference on the Same Principal?

Compare simple and compound interest at a fixed nominal annual rate, with a choice of how often interest compounds within the year.

Updated · Rules checked: 2026-09-16 · How calculations work

Enter the calculation inputs

Check against an example

10,000 yuan at an annual rate of 5% for 2 years, compounding once a year: with simple interest the total is 11,000 yuan; with compound interest it is 11,025 yuan.

Is the return really reinvested?

Simple interest is charged only on the original principal; compound interest adds each period's return to the principal. The compound model only matches reality if the returns genuinely keep taking part in interest calculations. If the interest has been withdrawn and spent, it cannot count as principal for later periods.

Formulas and rate conventions

Simple interest total = P × (1 + r × t). Compound interest total = P × (1 + r ÷ m)^(m×t). Here r is the nominal annual rate as a decimal, and m is the number of compoundings per year. The effective annual yield is not the same as the nominal annual rate — do not mix them up when entering values.

Which costs are not included

By default there are no additional deposits, redemptions, taxes or management fees, and the rate stays the same throughout. Fractional-year terms use a mathematical power extension, which does not represent how a bank accrues interest for a partial period. Results are scenario calculations and do not predict investment returns.

Stretch the term and the gap shows

100000 yuan at a nominal annual rate of 3% for 10 years: the simple-interest total is 130000 yuan; compounding once a year gives 134391.64 yuan; compounding once a month gives 134935.35 yuan. The difference between the two compound results comes from frequency: a nominal 3% compounded monthly corresponds to an effective annual rate of about 3.0416%. The longer the term and the higher the frequency, the wider the gap between compound and simple interest. In short scenarios of under two years, the gap is usually small enough to ignore — getting the principal and the rate right matters more.

Frequently asked questions

How do I tell the nominal rate from the effective annual rate? The nominal rate is the annual rate written in the contract; the effective annual rate folds in how often interest compounds within the year. Convert both products to effective annual rates before comparing them — going by the nominal figures alone can mislead you when compounding is frequent.

Can I use this tool to understand a loan? The principle is the same: interest on an outstanding balance also rolls into the balance and produces a compound effect. But a mortgage is repaid monthly and the balance falls month by month, so use the period-by-period schedule of the loan calculator — do not apply this page's lump-sum formula directly.

Why does my result differ from the product page? The product page may deduct management fees or taxes, or use a different day count and partial-period handling. This page is a scenario model with no fees and an unchanging rate; its results do not predict the return of any investment.

Adult BMI: calculate the number first, then check where it applies

Calculate BMI from height and weight. The page states clearly that it uses the WHO adult categories and does not output a diagnosis or a weight-loss prescription.

Updated and rules checked: 2026-09-16 · Calculation notes

Check with an example

Height 170 cm, weight 65 kg: 65 ÷ 1.7² ≈ 22.49. Centimeters must first be divided by 100 to get meters.

The result is only a screening indicator

BMI relates weight to the square of height; it does not directly measure body fat percentage or muscle mass. Two people with the same BMI can still have different body compositions. Do not judge personal health from this number alone, and do not design a treatment plan based on it.

Which categories this page uses

This page uses the WHO general adult categories: below 18.5; 18.5 to below 25; 25 to below 30; and 30 and above. Different countries or populations may use different screening cutoffs; the two must not be mixed. The category is determined by the unrounded result. Source: WHO information on adult nutrition and BMI, checked on 2026-09-16.

When the categories do not apply directly

Children and adolescents need standards based on age and sex, and situations such as pregnancy cannot use these categories directly either. Interpretation may also differ for athletes or people with high muscle mass. If you have personal health questions, rely on a professional assessment rather than this single number.

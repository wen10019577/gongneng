Appliance Electricity Budget: Running and Standby Counted Separately

Estimate electricity use and cost over a chosen number of days, from the daily running hours, the standby power drawn the rest of the time, and a price you enter yourself.

Check with an example

1,000 W running 2 hours a day and standing by at 5 W for the other 22 hours uses 63.3 kWh in 30 days. At 0.6 yuan per kWh, the estimated cost is 37.98 yuan.

Watts and kilowatt-hours are not the same thing

A watt is power; a kilowatt-hour is energy, and 1 kilowatt-hour is one unit of electricity (in China it is commonly called one "degree" of electricity). Running energy = power (watts) ÷ 1000 × hours per day × days; standby energy is calculated over the remaining 24 − running hours.

Rated power is not necessarily average power

Air conditioners, refrigerators and similar appliances cycle on and off or vary their output, so calculating continuously at the nameplate maximum can overstate usage. A closer estimate uses the average running power measured with a plug-in meter, or the appliance's measured consumption. Periods when the appliance is unplugged must not also be counted at standby power.

Why your bill may differ

This page calculates at a single flat price. It does not include the tiered residential pricing used in China, peak/off-peak time-of-use rates, base charges, or the electricity used by other appliances. 30 days is only an example; change it to 28, 31 or another number to match the actual period. The price is entered by you and does not represent the official tariff in your area.

How Is Tiered Electricity Pricing Calculated? 5,000 kWh a Year — Where the Extra 342 Yuan Goes

The rule of tiered electricity pricing can be stated in one sentence: the more you use, the higher the unit price on the portion above each threshold. But most people can't say which tier their household falls into, or exactly how much extra they pay. This guide works out three complete bills under one common set of tiers, then answers the frequent summer question along the way: how much does running the air conditioner overnight actually cost?

Written by Ernzi; worked examples re-checked · Updated: 2026-11-04

Tier thresholds and prices vary greatly by locality. This guide demonstrates the method with one common scheme: tier 1 is annual usage up to 2,160 kWh at 0.4883 yuan per kWh; the portion from 2,161 to 4,200 kWh carries a surcharge of 0.05 yuan per kWh; the portion above 4,200 kWh carries a surcharge of 0.30 yuan per kWh. Substitute your local power company's standards before applying it; in areas billed monthly, replace "year" with "month" and the same logic holds.

The rule: only the portion above the threshold is charged more

The most common misunderstanding is “once you pass 2,160 kWh, all your electricity is billed at the tier-2 price”. It isn’t. Tiers are priced in segments: the first 2,160 kWh are always at the tier-1 price, the part between 2,160 and 4,200 kWh is at the tier-2 price, and only what goes beyond that is at the tier-3 price. It works like the progressive rates in income tax (for how pay is calculated, see the guide on cumulative withholding): crossing a tier affects only the new slice, never the part already used.

Two observations. First, the tier-2 surcharge is actually very mild: 3,000 kWh costs only 42 yuan more, an average rise of 1.4 fen per kWh — no need to fret about “entering tier 2”. Second, tier 3 is the real watershed: of the 5,000 kWh, only 800 kWh falls into tier 3, yet the total bill rises by 342 yuan — 240 yuan of it from the 0.30 yuan surcharge on those 800 kWh. If your home has an electric water heater, electric underfloor heating, more than one air conditioner, or a home EV charging pile, first compare your cumulative usage and see how far you are from tier 3. Enter your household’s tiers and usage into this site’s kWh and electricity bill estimator and it will give you the segmented amounts directly.

How much does an air conditioner cost overnight?

A 1.5 HP variable-frequency air conditioner (HP, or “pi”, is the size rating commonly used in China) has a rated input power of about 1.1 kW, but once it reaches the set temperature it runs at reduced frequency, so actual consumption over a whole night is usually between 4 and 6 kWh (higher with a large room, a low set temperature, or a particularly hot night outside). Estimating at about 5 kWh (in China, 1 kWh is colloquially called one “degree” of electricity):

So “a few yuan for the air conditioner overnight” is not in itself scary. What is scary is the stacking effect: the few hundred extra kWh the air conditioner uses over three summer months can push the whole household into tier 3 by autumn, after which every kWh costs 0.30 yuan more. The right order of judgement is: first look at the year’s cumulative usage and your current tier, then look at the single appliance.

The bill suddenly gets more expensive at year-end — don’t blame the meter first

In areas billed annually, a year-end price rise usually has three layers stacked together: first, cumulative usage crosses into tier 2 or tier 3 and the unit price steps up; second, winter heating equipment itself uses more electricity (oil-filled radiators and fan heaters running, electric water heaters heating more often); third, in some areas the winter meter-reading cycle or the conventions for collected fees differ. How to check: compare the “cumulative electricity used this year” on your bill or in the app against the local tier table — confirm the tier first, then check whether this month’s usage is abnormal. Only abnormal usage (numbers still ticking over while nobody is home, for instance) calls for asking the power company to test the meter.

A further question: peak/valley pricing — is it worth it for night-owl households?

Many areas let you opt into peak/valley time-of-use pricing: at peak times the unit price is about 0.03 yuan above the tier-1 price, and in the valley (generally at night) about 0.20 yuan below (demonstrated with one common convention). Take a household using 300 kWh a month, 60% of it at night (air conditioner on overnight, EV charged overnight, water heater heating on a timer):

Pricing methodCalculationMonthly bill
Flat rate300 × 0.4883146.49 yuan
Peak/valleyPeak 120 × 0.5183 + valley 180 × 0.2883 = 62.20 + 51.89114.09 yuan

That saves 32.40 yuan a month, about 389 yuan a year. But the reverse also holds: a household that mainly uses electricity in the daytime (elderly people at home, an electric water heater keeping water hot all day) may pay more after switching to peak/valley — every extra slice of peak share lets the 0.03 yuan surcharge eat into the valley’s cheapness. The criterion is whether your valley usage share can stay above half — home EV charging, storage water heaters and washing machines on timers are natural valley loads. Before applying, check the time-of-use usage statistics in the power company’s app; in most areas you cannot switch back for a period once you’ve changed.

FAQ

Is tiered electricity pricing calculated monthly or annually?

Most areas settle annually; some settle monthly or by meter-reading cycle. With annual settlement it is normal for electricity to be cheap at the start of the year and expensive at the end. The announcement of your local power company governs.

How much does an air conditioner cost overnight?

A 1.5 HP variable-frequency air conditioner uses about 4–6 kWh over a whole night. At the tier-1 price, about 5 kWh × 0.4883 ≈ 2.44 yuan; if you are already in tier 3, about 3.94 yuan. Run it for 30 nights and that’s about 150 kWh, about 73 yuan at the tier-1 price.

Why doesn’t my bill show the tier?

Some areas’ bills show only the total usage and total amount; the tier breakdown is in the annual bill in the power company’s app. Judge which tier you are in by comparing the year’s cumulative usage against the local tier standard.

Notes

The prices and tiers in this guide are a demonstration convention. Residential electricity prices in each locality are set by the provincial price authority, and peak/valley pricing, combined-meter households and multi-person household allowances have their own rules — the publications of your local power company govern.

Housing Fund Loan vs Commercial Loan: How Much Do You Save? 1 Million Yuan over 20 Years: 503 Less per Month, 120,000 Less Interest

Money goes into your housing fund account every month — what is it actually worth when you buy a home? This guide works the difference out to the last digit: for the same 1 million yuan borrowed over 20 years with equal monthly payments, a housing fund loan costs about 120,000 yuan less in total interest than a commercial loan. That is the whole reason behind 'use the housing fund first if you have it'.

Written by Ernzi; worked examples re-checked · Updated: 2026-11-04

This article is an illustrative calculation: the housing fund rate uses the post-adjustment 2025 rate of 2.6% for a first home over 5 years as the assumed basis, and the commercial loan uses 3.6% as the assumed basis. Both are examples and do not represent the rate you will actually be approved for. Housing fund loan limits, home-count classification and rates are subject to your local housing fund management center and bank approval.

This is another example of rate sensitivity: in the equal-payment vs equal-principal guide the same effect came from the repayment method; here it comes from the loan type, and it is much bigger. So the order of decisions is clear: first check whether you can use a housing fund loan and how much you can borrow with it, then talk about repayment methods.

Not enough limit: how to structure a combination loan

Housing fund loans have a maximum limit (set by each city based on your contribution base, account balance, repayment capacity and home count; family limits commonly run from several hundred thousand to over a million yuan), so for an expensive home the fund loan often cannot cover the full amount. The standard solution is a combination loan, and the principle is one sentence: max out the low-rate portion and borrow as little as possible at the high rate.

An example structure: total borrowing need of 1.5 million yuan, and your city’s maximum family housing fund loan is 1 million yuan. Then 1 million goes through the housing fund (2.6% assumed basis) and 500,000 through a commercial loan (3.6% assumed basis). Calculate each monthly payment separately and add them; the terms can be kept the same for easier management. Later, if you have spare cash for early repayment, you would generally repay the commercial portion first (higher rate) and let the housing fund portion run — the ordering question has a fuller checklist in the early repayment guide; confirm the combination loan’s repayment-order rules with the bank before you apply.

Three things to confirm before using a housing fund loan

  1. Contribution eligibility: most cities require a minimum number of consecutive contribution months (commonly 6–12 months) and that you are still contributing when you apply. Whether contributions made in another city can be used locally varies by policy — ask the housing fund center first.
  2. Home count and rate: a second home gets a higher housing fund rate and a lower limit; if your family still has an outstanding housing fund loan, the classification rules for borrowing again differ by city.
  3. Disbursement speed and developer acceptance: housing fund loans (especially combination loans) are usually approved and disbursed more slowly than pure commercial loans. When buying a new home, confirm the developer accepts them and whether that affects your signing discount — count the time cost into the total as well.

How the limit is calculated: the balance-multiplier method

Each city uses a different formula. A common one is the ‘balance multiplier’ method: borrowable limit ≈ account balance × a multiplier (commonly 10–20×), then capped by the local maximum limit and an affordability assessment — the lowest of the three applies. Example: a couple’s combined account balance is 50,000 yuan, the local multiplier is 20×, and the family maximum is 1 million yuan, so the balance-based figure is exactly 1 million yuan and they are approved at the cap. If the balance were only 30,000 yuan, they could borrow only about 600,000 yuan, with the rest topped up by a commercial loan (the combination loan from the previous section). This leads to two actionable conclusions: first, do not withdraw from your housing fund casually in the year or two before buying — a withdrawal directly cuts your limit; second, if your balance is short, work out both halves of the combination loan’s payments in advance and treat ‘how much the housing fund can lend’ as a constraint on your total budget, not a surprise discovered at signing. The exact formula and multiplier are as published by your local housing fund center.

FAQ

What is the housing fund loan rate right now?

It follows the unified rates published by the central bank and has nothing to do with the LPR. After the May 2025 cut, a first home over 5 years is about 2.6%, with second homes correspondingly higher; the rate actually applied is the one enforced by your local housing fund management center.

What if the housing fund loan is not enough?

Use a combination loan: borrow up to the limit with the housing fund loan and cover the gap with a commercial loan. When repaying early, repaying the commercial portion first is generally better value; confirm the order with the bank.

How many years of contributions is the saving worth?

On this article’s example, 1 million yuan over 20 years saves about 120776 yuan in total interest and about 503 yuan per month. The fuller you use the limit and the longer the term, the more you save.

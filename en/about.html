About Ernzi

Everyday tools and guides whose calculation process you can check, rather than a single bare number. Every article here gives a complete worked example, so you can follow it step by step with a calculator.

Updated: 2026-11-04

What this site offers

Ernzi is built around one small everyday task: getting the numbers straight. The content comes in four groups, each pairing guide articles with calculators:

  • Pay and income tax: take-home pay estimates and total annual income tax, with articles that explain the cumulative withholding method, such as "Why does take-home pay start shrinking mid-year?" (in Chinese);
  • Loans and interest: fixed-rate loan estimates and comparisons of simple and compound interest, with item-by-item comparison articles such as "What is the real difference between equal installments and equal principal?" (in Chinese);
  • Shopping and saving: comparing discount offers, unit conversion and unit-price comparison, with breakdowns of real scenarios such as discount thresholds and topping up an order to reach free shipping;
  • Splitting bills and everyday topics: splitting group meals and travel costs, date calculations and electricity budgeting, with explanations of easy-to-miscalculate scenarios such as settling money that someone fronted.

All tools require no registration; calculations run in your browser and your inputs are not uploaded (see the Privacy Notice).

How content is produced and checked

  1. Definitions first, then the tool: before any tool goes live, its formulas, units, rounding method and scope are written up in a page note (for example, the tax tables come from the schedules to the Individual Income Tax Law, and the BMI categories from the WHO). A tool whose definitions cannot be stated clearly does not go live.
  2. Every worked example can be recomputed: each number in the guide articles has been checked with the tool itself or by an independent hand calculation, and the examples deliberately use teaching numbers that divide evenly and are easy to compare, so readers can verify them step by step.
  3. Dates are shown: tool pages carry an "updated and rules checked" date, and articles an update date. When external rules change, we re-check the affected pages and update the dates.
  4. No exaggeration: a feature that has not been verified is never called "professionally reviewed" or "officially certified". The scope of our testing and our known limitations are stated as they are in the Calculation and Maintenance Notes.

Where this site came from

In its early days, this site reused the code of a tool project called CalcSmart. Starting in September 2026, it was systematically overhauled: all pages and calculation definitions were reworked, features such as discount-order comparison, settling fronted payments when splitting bills, and strict base parsing were added, and every tool gained a recomputable example and a statement of scope. From November 2026, the site's focus shifted from a "collection of tools" to "guide articles + tools": first explain the scenario and the rules, then provide the calculator. The site's code is maintained in a public GitHub repository.

Corrections and feedback

If you find a calculation result that does not match its explanation, a number in an article that does not add up, or a rule that has changed, your feedback is welcome. Following the format set out on the Contact and corrections page, please provide the tool's URL, the input values and the expected result (you can reproduce the problem with fictional amounts — do not send sensitive information such as ID numbers or bank card numbers). Confirmed errors are corrected, and the correction is reflected in the page's update date.

How the two repayment methods differ

Equal principal splits the principal evenly across all periods, and interest falls as the balance falls, so payments start higher. Under a fixed rate, equal payment keeps the total the same every period, but interest makes up a larger share early on. Compare the two with the same principal, number of periods and rate — not by the first month's payment alone.

Formulas, and the zero-rate case

Monthly rate r = annual rate ÷ 12; number of periods n; principal P. The equal-payment amount is P × r ÷ [1 − (1+r)^-n]. When r is 0, use P ÷ n directly to avoid dividing by zero. Under equal principal, each period's principal is P ÷ n, and interest is the opening balance × r.

The schedule vs. a real statement

Each row of the table is shown to the cent, while the internal calculation keeps more precision, so the displayed columns may not add up exactly because of rounding. Real banks may round period by period, accrue interest daily, adjust the rate, or set a final-period adjustment. This page does not cover early repayment, fees, combined loans or rate repricing, and does not treat the example rate as a current market quote.

Scale it up to a real mortgage to see the difference

Change the principal to 1000000 yuan, the periods to 240, and assume an annual rate of 3.6% (a calculation assumption only, not a market quote): equal payment is 5851.11 yuan per period with total interest of 404267.52 yuan; equal principal starts at 7166.67 yuan and ends at 4179.17 yuan, with total interest of 361500 yuan — a difference of 42767.52 yuan. Frequently asked questions

Why does equal payment cost more interest overall? Because less principal is repaid early on, the balance falls more slowly, and interest is charged on the balance period by period, so the total is naturally higher. It is not the bank overcharging — the schedule can be verified line by line.

Is early repayment worth it, and when should I do it? What you repay early is principal, and interest afterwards is charged straight away on the lower balance — there is no such thing as "paying the interest first". Before deciding, check your contract's early-repayment penalty, minimum amounts and frequency limits, then compare other uses for the money.

Can I use this page for a floating-rate loan? This page is a fixed-rate model. LPR-linked loans reprice every year and have to be calculated in segments, using the new rate and the balance at the time; results here are only an approximation.

Can I switch repayment methods mid-loan? Most banks do not allow switching between repayment methods — check your contract. A common alternative is to stay on equal payment and make partial early repayments.

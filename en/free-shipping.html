Adding an Item Just to Get Free Shipping: Are You Saving, or Spending More?

'Saved 8 yuan of shipping' sounds like a saving, but you may have paid 10 yuan extra to get it. Whether padding an order is worth it depends on a single difference. This guide works three complete orders down to the last yuan, then adds the break-even maths for membership free shipping.

Written by Ernzi; worked examples re-checked · Updated: 2026-11-04

The figures in this article are teaching examples constructed by this site and do not represent any merchant's promotion or a real user's bill. You can re-check each step and then substitute your own data. Thresholds and shipping rules vary by merchant; the checkout page governs.

First, set the ‘no padding’ baseline

Your basket is 89 yuan, below the 99 yuan threshold, so shipping is 8 yuan. Without padding you pay 89 + 8 = 97 yuan. That 97 yuan is the baseline. There are two common ways to get this wrong: comparing the padding item’s price with 0 (‘it’s only 10 yuan’), or fixating on ‘saved 8 yuan of shipping’ and assuming you saved 8 yuan. The right comparison is always the difference between two complete order totals.

With the 10 yuan item, the extra cash you pay is 2 yuan (= 10 − 8), in exchange for an item priced at 10 yuan. Whether it is worth 2 yuan depends on whether you actually need it: a household staple already on your shopping list is obviously good value at 2 yuan; a random item grabbed just to pad the order is a waste even at 2 yuan extra. The 6 yuan case is the worst outcome: it never crossed the threshold, the shipping is still charged in full, and you simply spent 6 yuan more. The first check in padding an order is always ‘did adding it actually reach the threshold?’ — if the shipping on the checkout page has not turned to 0, either you did not reach it or that item does not participate.

When padding genuinely reduces your total spend

Change the basket to 95 yuan, everything else unchanged: without padding you pay 95 + 8 = 103 yuan; add a 4 yuan item to reach 99 yuan and you pay 99 yuan — this order’s cash outlay drops by a net 4 yuan, and you get an extra item as well. The general rule:

The extra cost of padding = the padding item's price − the shipping waived. When the price is less than the shipping, your total spend falls; when it is more, you are 'paying the difference for an extra item'; when it exactly equals the shipping, your total is unchanged and the item is effectively free (provided you will use it).

Now a larger order: basket 242 yuan, free-shipping threshold 288 yuan, shipping 10 yuan. Without padding you pay 252 yuan. The gap is 46 yuan — adding a 45.9 yuan item looks like it misses by only 0.1 yuan, and indeed the total of 287.9 yuan still falls short of the threshold, so you pay 297.9 yuan in total, more than not padding at all. Add instead a 49.9 yuan item you were going to buy anyway: the total reaches 291.9 yuan with free shipping — 39.9 yuan more than not padding, but that item bought separately would cost 49.9 yuan (possibly plus another shipping charge), so buying it early actually saves. The larger the gap, the more you should pick padding items only from your ‘was going to buy anyway’ list, rather than grabbing something at random from the padding suggestions.

Three knock-on traps

Padding can push a threshold discount up — or off. A padding item might push the order into a higher threshold-discount tier (a pleasant surprise), or, by joining a different promotion, change your existing offers. Before and after padding, compare the checkout-page totals of the two complete orders; do not calculate only the shipping line. For how the discount order itself is calculated, see threshold discount or percentage off first.

Return a padding item and the shipping and discounts get clawed back. Do not treat ‘pad now, return later’ as a saving strategy: after a return the order amount is recalculated, and shipping for a missed free-shipping threshold and discounts for a missed threshold are normally clawed back under the rules; doing it repeatedly can also affect your account standing. Accounting on the items you finally keep is the real spend.

How the free-shipping threshold is judged. Some merchants judge it on the items’ original price, some on the amount after coupons, some on the amount paid; remote areas, overweight parcels and split shipments can attract surcharges. Before padding, verify on the checkout page that the shipping really drops to zero, then pay.

While we are here: is membership free shipping worth it?

The same logic judges e-commerce memberships: suppose the fee is 99 yuan a year, shipping is 6 yuan per order, and members get free shipping on every order. Break-even orders = 99 ÷ 6 = 16.5, i.e. you need at least 17 orders a year to break even. Below that frequency, paying shipping or padding occasionally is better value. Member-only coupons and discounts bundled with the membership should be assessed separately and counted only if you will definitely use them — do not count ‘might use’ perks twice alongside the free shipping.

Four things to check before ordering

  1. Is the threshold judged on the original price or the post-discount amount? This article and the tools assume the item amount you enter is the amount the threshold is judged on; adjust it to the merchant’s rules.
  2. Does the padding item participate in free shipping? Items that do not participate cannot count toward the threshold amount.
  3. Are there remote-area, overweight or split-shipment shipping surcharges? This article calculates a single fixed shipping charge.
  4. Does padding change another threshold-discount tier? If so, work out both real order totals separately and compare.

FAQ

How do I judge whether padding for free shipping is worth it?

Compare one number only: the padding item’s price minus the shipping waived. If the result is positive, you paid that much extra cash today for an item — worth it only if you need it; if it is negative or zero, padding genuinely reduced this order’s spend. This assumes padding actually crosses the threshold and does not change other threshold discounts.

If I return the padding item, do I keep the free shipping for free?

Normally not. After a return the order amount is recalculated, and if the free-shipping or threshold-discount threshold is no longer met, the shipping and discounts are clawed back under the rules; doing this frequently can also affect your account standing. Do not plan returns as a saving strategy.

Is the free-shipping threshold judged before or after discounts?

It varies by merchant; common practice is to judge it on the amount of items participating in the promotion, or on the amount actually paid. Before ordering, confirm on the checkout page that the shipping has become 0; if adding an item did not change the shipping, check the threshold basis and whether that item participates in free shipping.

How many orders a year make membership free shipping pay for itself?

Divide the annual fee by the per-order shipping. With a 99 yuan fee and 6 yuan shipping per order, you need about 17 orders to break even (99 ÷ 6 = 16.5). Order less often than that, and paying shipping or padding is better value.

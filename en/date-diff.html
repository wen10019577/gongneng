Date difference: how many days apart, and should the end date count?

Calculate the interval between two dates in calendar days, clearly distinguishing "days apart" from "counting both the first and the last day".

Updated and rules checked: 2026-09-16 · Calculation notes

Enter the inputs

Check with an example

September 6 to September 13 is 7 days apart. If you check in every day and both the first and the last day count, that is 8 days.

Hotel stays are usually counted by the date interval ("nights"); for how many days an event lasts, first confirm whether both ends are included.

First confirm what you actually want to count

From Monday to Tuesday you cross one date boundary, so the interval is 1 day. If the question is "classes are held on both days — how many days of class in total?", both dates must be counted, and the answer is 2 days. The checkbox changes only this counting convention; it does not add or remove weekends.

Worked examples you can verify by hand

  • Same day to same day: 0 days by default; 1 day when both ends are counted.
  • 2024-02-28 to 2024-03-01: 2 days by default, because that year has a February 29.
  • 2025-02-28 to 2025-03-01: 1 day by default.

Formula and boundaries

Result = the difference in Gregorian calendar days between the end date and the start date; add 1 when both ends are counted. The calculation uses uniform date boundaries, so daylight saving time cannot turn a day into 23 or 25 hours. It does not express the number of hours between two specific moments, and it does not automatically apply contractual or legal deadline rules. If the end date is earlier than the start date, you will be prompted to swap the order.

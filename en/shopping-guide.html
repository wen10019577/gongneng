How Do Percentage Discounts, Threshold Discounts and Shipping Actually Stack? A 299 Yuan Basket Paid Three Ways

Discount rules are experts at creating illusions: 10% off sounds bigger than 30 yuan off, yet stacked threshold discounts often win. This guide calculates all three combinations for the same basket to the end, then swaps in a slightly cheaper basket — and the winner instantly reverses. The conclusion: offers have no fixed ranking, only the final amount your particular basket actually pays.

Written by Ernzi; worked examples re-checked · Updated: 2026-11-04

This article uses illustrative rules: the basket is 299 yuan; the available offers are one 10%-off coupon (pay 90%), a store offer of 40 off over 299, and a platform coupon of 30 off over 200 (the platform coupon's threshold is judged on the amount currently payable); shipping is free over 99 yuan, otherwise 6 yuan. Each platform's real rules are as shown on its checkout page; this article teaches the general comparison method. The ordering question for threshold discounts and percentage discounts is taken apart in more detail in the discount-order guide.

Three paths, 40 yuan apart

PathStep-by-stepPaid (incl. shipping)
① Store threshold discount + platform coupon299 − 40 = 259 (still over 200) → 259 − 30 = 229, free shipping229.00 yuan
② 10%-off coupon + platform coupon299 × 0.9 = 269.10 (still over 200) → 269.10 − 30 = 239.10, free shipping239.10 yuan
③ 10%-off coupon only299 × 0.9 = 269.10, free shipping269.10 yuan

Why did the flat reductions beat the percentage? A threshold discount cuts the base directly: the store coupon removes 40 first, the remaining 259 still comfortably clears the platform coupon’s threshold, and another 30 comes off — 70 yuan in total. The 10% off looks grand but actually saves only 29.90 yuan: a percentage discount’s real value = original price × (1 − the rate you pay), and when the original price is not large, it simply cannot match a decent threshold coupon. This is also why big-sale pages love headlining percentage offers: the psychological punch of a percentage far exceeds the money it is actually worth.

Swap in a 210 yuan basket and the winner reverses

Change the basket to 210 yuan (all other rules unchanged; the store’s 40 off over 299 no longer applies):

PathStep-by-stepPaid
Platform coupon only210 − 30 = 180, free shipping180.00 yuan
10%-off coupon only210 × 0.9 = 189 (now below 200, the platform coupon is voided), free shipping189.00 yuan

The reversal turns on the threshold: applying the 10% off first squeezes the amount down to 189 and knocks out the 30-off-over-200 platform coupon, while using the coupon directly takes a steady 30 off. This explains why ‘percentage first, amount off second’ is sometimes a trap — the percentage discount not only gives limited savings, it can casually void your next coupon. The right order of judgement is: first list every combination that can actually take effect, then calculate what each one makes you pay — never rank offers by how big their names sound.

A 30-second self-check before checkout

  1. Expand the cost breakdown and confirm each coupon’s status is ‘in effect’, not ‘selected but threshold not met’.
  2. Look at the shipping line: for an order that drops below the free-shipping threshold after discounts, add the shipping back into the amount paid before comparing (for the padding trade-off, see the free-shipping padding guide).
  3. Calculate the final amount paid for each candidate combination — cross-check with this site’s discount calculator and threshold-discount tool instead of doing mental arithmetic on the checkout page.
  4. Watch for items marked ‘not participating in the promotion’: they count toward the original price but may not count toward some coupons’ threshold base — the number-one reason checkout totals do not reconcile (for how to compare unit prices, see the unit price guide).

Pre-sale deposit ‘expansion’: 50 counts as 100, but the real saving is only 5%

Another big-sale device is the expanding deposit: pay a 50 yuan deposit and it counts as 100 yuan, which sounds like half price. Run an item priced at 999 yuan through the whole flow: pay the 50 yuan deposit first; the balance = 999 − 100 (the expanded deposit credit) = 899 yuan; total actually paid 949 yuan. The real saving = 999 − 949 = 50 yuan, equivalent to 5% — a completely different story from the literal punch of ‘50 counts as 100’. In essence it is just a ‘50 off over 999’ coupon, with two extra costs attached: the deposit is usually non-refundable (the cost of changing your mind), and if you forget to pay the balance in the payment window, the deposit is forfeited. The judging method does not change: add up everything you actually hand over, compare it with the list price, and the difference is the discount; for any staged-payment pitch, merge it into a single bill before evaluating.

FAQ

Which should I use first, a percentage discount or a threshold discount?

There is no fixed answer; it depends on the threshold basis and your order amount. In the 299 yuan basket, stacked threshold discounts save the most (229 yuan); in the 210 yuan basket, a single threshold coupon beats discounting first. The only reliable method is to calculate the final amount paid (including shipping) for every usable combination and compare those.

Why does the checkout page’s discount differ from my calculation?

Three common reasons: the threshold is judged on the discounted price, so a coupon fails; some items do not participate in the promotion; and shipping is recalculated after the discounts and falls back into a charged band. Before checkout, expand the cost breakdown and check it line by line.

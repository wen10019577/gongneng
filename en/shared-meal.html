Someone Doesn't Drink — How Do You Split a Group Dinner Bill Fairly? A 620 Yuan Bill Broken Down

Four people share a meal with a total bill of 620 yuan. Split it evenly and it's 155 yuan each — but the two who didn't drink are probably thinking: that 160 yuan of alcohol, I didn't touch a drop. The way to split a bill so everyone feels it's fair is to divide it by consumption circles: what's shared is split by everyone, what's within a circle is split by that circle. This guide splits the 620 yuan down to each person, then handles two common variables: discounts and someone leaving early.

Written by Ernzi; worked examples re-checked · Updated: 2026-11-04

The examples in this guide are teaching scenarios, and the split follows whatever the participants agreed beforehand. Once the shares are worked out, settle who transfers what to whom with the netting-method guide; for the general principles when several people front several payments, see the guide on settling a group meal.

Step 1: divide the bill into circles by consumption

CircleAmountBorne byShare each
Dishes and staples (shared circle)460 yuanAll 4 people115 yuan
Alcohol (drinks circle)160 yuanThe 2 who drank80 yuan

Each person’s amount due: the two non-drinkers pay 115 yuan each; the two drinkers pay 115 + 80 = 195 yuan each. Self-check: 115 × 2 + 195 × 2 = 620 yuan, matching the total bill. The same circling can be refined further: the sashimi one person ordered alone goes to that person, a child’s kids’ meal goes to the parent — the split is as precise as the level of granularity you agree for the circles. But don’t go as fine as a single bite of a dish: a bill calculated too finely makes for a tiring meal.

Step 2: spread a discount back into each circle proportionally

Suppose the table used a “50 off over 500” deal and actually paid 570 yuan. The discount applies to the whole bill, so scale everyone’s share proportionally: scaling factor = 570 ÷ 620 ≈ 0.9194.

PersonOriginal shareShare after discount
Non-drinkers (×2)115 yuanAbout 105.7 yuan
Drinkers (×2)195 yuanAbout 179.3 yuan

105.7 × 2 + 179.3 × 2 = 570 yuan — it closes exactly (rounding to the jiao, with the odd remainder absorbed by the payer or the organizer). If instead it was a “drinks-only voucher”, then only the drinks circle is reduced: the part of the drinks actually paid that drops benefits only the drinkers, and must not be spread to the non-drinkers — a discount follows the circle it hits. If A, one of the drinkers, fronted the full 570 yuan, the other three transfer him 105.7, 105.7 and 179.3 yuan respectively to settle up; you can enter the items into this site’s group meal splitting tool and get the transfer list directly.

Two frequent variables

Someone leaves early. A person who leaves early settles by the circles they took part in before leaving: drinks opened after they left don’t enter their share. The easiest approach is for them to snap a photo of the bill as it stands when they leave, or transfer a round sum on the spot and square up afterwards against the final circles — stating the rule in advance beats digging through chat history later.

Someone clearly ate much less. There is no rule that says “two bites means 70% off”, but regular dinner groups can have customary tiers: young children who only had soup aren’t counted, for instance, or light eaters count as half a share. Splitting tools solve the arithmetic; agreements solve the feelings: a rule stated before ordering is far cheaper than one stated when the bill arrives. For merging several days of expenses on a trip, see the trip-splitting guide.

Which circle do tea charges and service charges belong to?

A few odds and ends on the bill are easy to argue about; the circling principle is the same as for dishes — they follow whatever caused them. Tea/seat charges are levied per head and belong to the shared circle — whoever is seated has a share: 4 people × 5 yuan = 20 yuan into the shared circle, split evenly. If the service charge is a percentage of the whole bill (commonly 10%), split it back into the circles proportionally: 46 yuan of service charge corresponding to the 460 yuan of food goes into the shared circle, 16 yuan corresponding to the 160 yuan of drinks goes into the drinks circle, and each circle’s per-person share rises proportionally — the method doesn’t change. A corkage fee relates only to the bottle that was brought in, so it goes into the small circle of whoever ordered the drinks; takeaway boxes and extra dishes ordered to take home are booked to the person taking them away. State these four rules before paying and 90% of splitting friction disappears at the ordering stage.

FAQ

Why shouldn’t alcohol be split evenly among everyone?

The sense of fairness in splitting comes from “whoever consumes, bears it”. Making everyone share an item only some people consumed means the non-consumers are paying for someone else. Divide the bill into circles by item: shared items are split by everyone, circle items by the people in that circle, and adding the parts gives each person’s share.

How do I apportion discounts from threshold deals and group-buy vouchers?

A whole-bill discount scales each person’s share proportionally (actual paid ÷ total bill is the factor); a discount targeting only one category of dish reduces only the corresponding circle. Round remainders to the jiao, absorbed by the organizer.

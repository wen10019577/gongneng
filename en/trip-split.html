Someone Joins or Leaves a Trip Halfway — Who Shares Which Costs? A Four-Person, Five-Day Bill Worked Out

Four people go away for five days, and one of them doesn't arrive until day three — dividing the total bill by four is unfair to him; counting only his days risks missing shared expenses. The rule is actually just one sentence: <strong>each expense is shared only by the people who were there when it occurred</strong>. This guide splits lodging, transport and meals by that rule, and settles up in 3 transfers.

Written by Ernzi; worked examples re-checked · Updated: 2026-11-04

The example in this guide is a teaching scenario: A, B and D are present throughout, and C joins on day 3. Total spending is 6,000 yuan. When the fronting relationships get complicated, work out the shares by this guide first, then generate the transfers with the netting-method guide; for splitting a single group meal, see the meal-splitting guide.

Lodging: split by the night, counting who is checked in each night

Total lodging is 3,000 yuan over 4 nights, 750 yuan per night. Only 3 people are checked in for the first 2 nights; all 4 are there for the last 2 nights:

SegmentAmountBorne byEach
Lodging, first 2 nights1,500 yuanA, B, D500 yuan
Lodging, last 2 nights1,500 yuanA, B, C, D375 yuan

Lodging shares: A, B and D 875 yuan each, C 375 yuan. If the whole stay were split evenly, C would pay 750 yuan — the extra 375 yuan is exactly the two nights he didn’t stay. Almost every lodging dispute comes from this: don’t average the whole stay — split by the night first. Booking a whole holiday home in advance that can’t be partially refunded is an exception, to be written into the group’s agreement before booking (see the FAQ at the end).

Transport and meals: split by segment, with boundaries at joining/leaving points

Expense segmentAmountBorne byEach
Transport · early segment (charter vehicle, 3 people)300 yuanA, B, D100 yuan
Transport · later segment (4 people)600 yuanEveryone150 yuan
Meals · early segment (3 people)900 yuanA, B, D300 yuan
Meals · later segment (4 people)1,200 yuanEveryone300 yuan

Total share per person: A = 875 + 250 + 600 = 1,725 yuan; B and D are also 1,725 yuan each; C = 375 + 150 + 300 = 825 yuan. Self-check: 1,725 × 3 + 825 = 6,000 yuan, matching the total.

Final step: net fronted payments against shares — 3 transfers

Fronting in this example: A paid the 3,000 yuan lodging and the 900 yuan of early-segment meals, 3,900 yuan in total; B paid the 900 yuan of transport and 300 yuan of the later-segment meals, 1,200 yuan in total; after joining, C paid 900 yuan of the later-segment meals; D fronted nothing. Net = paid − share:

PersonFrontedShareNet
A3,9001,725+2,175 (collect)
B1,2001,725−525 (pay)
C900825+75 (collect)
D01,725−1,725 (pay)

Three transfers: D gives A 1,725 yuan, B gives A 450 yuan, B gives C 75 yuan — all settled. Enter each expense into this site’s trip-splitting tool with its participants marked, and the shares and net amounts are calculated automatically; the same applies to someone leaving halfway, except the segment boundary becomes the point where they left.

Another way to organize it: collect a group fund first, then refund or top up by share

With many people and frequent spending, fronting expense by expense is easy to under-record. You can invert it and use a common fund: before setting off, each person pays 1,500 yuan of group fund (4 people, 6,000 yuan in total) to one person to hold; all shared spending comes out of the fund, and along the way you only record “how much was spent, and on which segment”. At the end of the trip, still calculate the shares by the presence rule, then difference each share against the 1,500 yuan already paid in: A, B and D have shares of 1,725 yuan each and top up 225 yuan each; C’s share is 825 yuan and 675 yuan is refunded to him. Three transfers: A, B and D each transfer 225 yuan to C (if the fund holder is one of them, net it off within their own top-up or refund). The advantage of the fund model is that nobody has to keep transferring money to each other during the trip and the accounts have a single exit; the cost is that you need a holder everyone trusts and a ledger kept on the spot — if the record-keeping discipline collapses, no splitting method, however good, can save it.

FAQ

Does someone who joins halfway have to share the shared costs from before?

The principle is “you share only if you were there”: nights they didn’t stay, rides they didn’t take don’t enter their share. The exception is shared costs already incurred under the membership as it stood at booking time and non-refundable (such as a whole holiday home) — the splitting rule has to be stated clearly before booking, not added afterwards.

Why split lodging by the night rather than averaging the whole stay?

Because the number of people checked in differs by night. Splitting by the night puts the low-occupancy nights on the people who were there and the full nights on everyone; averaging the whole stay makes a late joiner pay for nights they didn’t stay — the most common source of disputes.

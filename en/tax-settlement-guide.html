Why Does Annual Reconciliation Give One Person a 1530 Refund and Another a 13320 Additional Payment?

Every March, some people show off their refund while others are startled by an additional payment. Neither is luck: the reconciliation simply squares the 'monthly withholding estimate' against the 'actual tax due for the year'. This guide uses two complete examples to work out where the difference comes from, down to the last digit.

Written by Ernzi; worked examples re-checked · Updated: 2026-11-04

The examples in this guide are constructed scenarios; the rates follow the annual rate table for comprehensive income in China's Individual Income Tax Law, and the withholding method is the cumulative withholding method. An actual reconciliation also has to consider reductions and exemptions, donations, foreign tax credits and the like, which this guide does not cover one by one; your result follows the reconciliation system's assessment. This page does not provide individual tax advice.

First, remember one formula

Reconciliation difference = actual tax payable for the year − tax already withheld for the year. Negative: refund. Positive: additional payment.

Why is withholding inaccurate? Three structural reasons: first, withholding estimates by cumulative progress and doesn’t know your final full-year income (for the principle, see the cumulative withholding guide); second, the basic deduction is given at 5000 yuan per month during withholding, but at reconciliation it’s given in full at 60000 yuan for the year — even if you only worked half the year; third, with income from multiple sources, each withholding agent can only see its own slice. The two examples below cover one typical case each.

Refund example: started work in July, only worked 6 months

Suppose someone starts work in July, with a monthly salary of 15000 yuan, monthly personal social insurance and housing fund of 1500 yuan, and special additional deductions of 1000 yuan. Over 6 months of employment, each month’s new taxable income = 15000 − 5000 − 1500 − 1000 = 7500 yuan, cumulating to 45000 yuan by December. Total withholding over the months comes to 1980 yuan (225 yuan in each of the first 4 months, 330 yuan in month 5, 750 yuan in month 6 — the bracket-jump process is the same as in the salary guide).

At reconciliation it’s recalculated for the full year: annual income 90000 yuan, minus the basic deduction given in full at 60000 yuan, minus social insurance of 9000 yuan and special additional deductions of 6000 yuan, leaving taxable income of only 15000 yuan, and actual tax = 15000 × 3% = 450 yuan.

The source of the 1530 yuan is perfectly clear: during withholding he only received 6 months of basic deduction (30000 yuan); at reconciliation it’s topped up to 60000 yuan, and the extra 30000 yuan of deduction presses taxable income down from 45000 yuan to 15000 yuan, with the rate also falling back from the 10% bracket to 3%. People who didn’t work the full year — starting partway through, or leaving with a gap mid-year — are the group most likely to get a refund.

Additional payment example: salaries from two employers, each withheld separately

Suppose someone works at two employers at the same time, each paying a monthly salary of 12000 yuan, with social insurance of 1000 yuan and special additional deductions of 500 yuan on each payslip (simplified to the basis actually claimed). Each employer treats him as someone with ‘only this one salary’: each month’s new taxable income is 5500 yuan, cumulating to 66000 yuan over the year, and each withholds 66000 × 10% − 2520 = 4080 yuan, for a combined 8160 yuan withheld.

Merged at reconciliation: annual income 288000 yuan, the basic deduction can only be enjoyed once at 60000 yuan, social insurance totals 24000 yuan and special additional deductions total 12000 yuan, so taxable income = 192000 yuan, entering the 20% bracket, and actual tax = 192000 × 20% − 16920 = 21480 yuan.

Nobody calculated wrong: each employer used the low brackets and the quick deduction once, but merged, those ‘allowances’ count only once, and the marginal rate jumps to 20% as well. People with multiple jobs, or a salary combined with remuneration for services, should estimate their full-year tax on merged income in advance and set aside cash for the additional payment — plug the merged figures into this site’s annual tax tool and you can see the number ahead of time. Switching the year-end bonus method at reconciliation also changes the difference; see the year-end bonus guide.

Self-check list before filing

  1. Is the income record complete: check every entry in the income and tax details in the Individual Income Tax app, especially former employers and remuneration for services; if you dispute an entry, appeal it before filing.
  2. Are the deductions fully claimed: special additional deductions (children’s education, support for elderly parents, housing, continuing education, major medical expenses) that were missed during withholding can be added at reconciliation — this is one of the most common sources of refunds.
  3. Try the bonus both ways: calculate separate taxation and merging, and confirm with the lower one.
  4. Is the bank card information right: refunds are paid to your own bank card; a wrong card number leaves the refund stuck at the review stage.
  5. Keep cash for an additional payment: if you expect to owe, set the money aside in advance and pay it before June 30 to avoid late fees.

FAQ

Where does the reconciliation refund money come from?

It’s your own money that was over-withheld. Withholding estimates by cumulative progress; the full-year assessment recalculates on actual income, the full 60000-yuan basic deduction and all deductions, and the part you overpaid is refunded to you. It’s essentially giving you your change, not a reward.

What situation most easily leads to an additional payment?

Income from multiple sources is the most typical: each withholding agent withholds only on its own slice and each enjoys the low brackets, but merged, the total enters a higher bracket. Next come cases such as the withholding rate on remuneration for services not matching the comprehensive rate, or the tax rising after switching the year-end bonus method.

If the additional payment is small, can I skip the reconciliation?

Under the current rules, cases such as annual comprehensive income not exceeding 120,000 yuan, or an additional payment not exceeding 400 yuan, can be exempt from filing the reconciliation. The specific conditions follow the reconciliation announcement of the year; refunds have no amount threshold — to get one, you must file.

When is the reconciliation filed?

Generally from March 1 to June 30 of the following year. It’s best not to leave it to the last few days; late additional payments may incur late fees, and the earlier a refund claim is filed, the faster it is usually processed.

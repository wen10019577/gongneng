Year-End Bonus: Taxed Separately or Merged with Salary? Two Income Levels, Worked Out

Every year when the year-end bonus is paid, someone asks this question, and the answer is always 'it depends which bracket you're in'. This guide gives no rule of thumb — it gives two complete, re-checkable examples: for the same kind of bonus, one person saves 2520 yuan by taxing it separately, another saves 600 yuan by merging it; and it ends with the blind-spot table where a bonus 1 yuan higher costs 2310 yuan more in tax.

Written by Ernzi; worked examples re-checked · Updated: 2026-11-04

The examples in this guide are constructed scenarios; the rates follow the annual rate table for comprehensive income in China's Individual Income Tax Law and the monthly-converted rate table for annual one-time bonuses. Under the current announcement, the separate-taxation policy for bonuses continues until December 31, 2027; base your own choice on the actual trial results of both methods in the reconciliation system. This page does not provide individual tax advice.

Where the two methods differ: the principle in one sentence

Taxed separately: the bonus stays out of comprehensive income. You look up the rate for it in the monthly-converted rate table using “bonus ÷ 12”, apply that rate to the full amount, and subtract only one month’s quick deduction. The advantage is that it doesn’t push your salary into a higher bracket; the cost is that it can’t use any deductions you haven’t used up.

Merged into comprehensive income: the bonus is added to your full-year salary; after subtracting the 60000-yuan basic deduction and all other deductions, the annual rate table is applied to the whole. The advantage is that it can absorb idle deductions; the cost is that the bigger total may pull everything into a higher bracket.

So the judgement comes down to one thing: whether you’ve used up your deductions, and how high your comprehensive income bracket is. The two examples below are exactly the two ends of the spectrum.

Example 1: the bracket is already fairly high — separate taxation saves 2520 yuan

Suppose the taxable income from the full year’s salary (after all deductions) is 60000 yuan, and the year-end bonus is 36000 yuan.

MethodCalculationTotal individual income tax for the year
Taxed separatelySalary: 60000 × 10% − 2520 = 3480; bonus: 36000 ÷ 12 = 3000, looked up as 3%, 36000 × 3% = 10804560 yuan
Merged(60000 + 36000) × 10% − 25207080 yuan

Separate taxation pays 2520 yuan less. The reason: on its own, the bonus gets the low 3% rate (in the monthly table, 3000 yuan sits exactly at the top of the 3% bracket), while merged, the whole 36000 yuan is taxed at the 10% marginal rate of his salary — the difference is exactly 36000 × (10% − 3%) = 2520 yuan. The higher the income and the higher the salary bracket, the bigger this gap.

Example 2: deductions not used up — merging saves 600 yuan instead

A different person: full-year salary income is not high, and after the 60000-yuan basic deduction and all other deductions, taxable income is −20000 yuan (i.e. 20000 yuan of deduction allowance went unused), with a year-end bonus of 30000 yuan.

MethodCalculationTotal individual income tax for the year
Taxed separatelySalary tax 0; bonus: 30000 ÷ 12 = 2500, looked up as 3%, 30000 × 3% = 900900 yuan
Merged−20000 + 30000 = 10000, 10000 × 3% = 300300 yuan

Merging pays 600 yuan less. Those 20000 yuan of idle deductions are worth nothing under separate taxation; merged, they first offset 20000 yuan of the bonus, leaving only 10000 yuan taxed at 3%. People who started work partway through the year, joined mid-year, or have large special additional deductions often sit in this range — for how their withholding goes during the year, see the cumulative withholding guide.

The blind spot: 1 yuan more bonus, 2310 yuan more tax

Separate taxation has one more trap you have to know about. When the bonus crosses a critical point, the full amount switches to the higher rate, while the quick deduction is still only one month’s worth — so just above each critical point there is a “blind spot” where the more you’re paid, the less you take home:

The bonus went up by 1 yuan, the tax went up by 2310.10 yuan, and take-home pay actually fell by 2309.10 yuan. The same critical points occur at 144000, 300000, 420000, 660000 and 960000 yuan, and the blind spot widens with each bracket. If the bonus your company sets lands just above a critical point, there are two proper solutions: bring the bonus down to the critical point (pay a few hundred less, take home two thousand more), or raise it past the blind spot. What to do

  1. First estimate which bracket your full-year salary taxable income falls into and whether your deductions are used up; compare with the two examples above to work out which end you’re closer to.
  2. If your bonus is near a critical point, check the blind spot first, then talk about which method to choose — the blind-spot loss is far bigger than the difference between the methods.
  3. Whichever method your employer withheld under, don’t panic: at the following year’s reconciliation, trial-calculate both methods in the system and confirm with the lower tax — this is the officially provided chance to change your mind. FAQ

Which is better for a year-end bonus: taxed separately or merged into comprehensive income?

It depends on the bracket of your annual taxable income. People whose comprehensive income bracket is already fairly high save more with separate taxation; people with low full-year income whose deductions aren’t used up save more by merging, because the idle deductions can offset part of the bonus. Calculate both, then choose.

What is the year-end bonus blind spot?

Under separate taxation, once the bonus crosses a critical point (36000, 144000, etc.) the full amount switches to a higher rate, so a slightly larger bonus can mean a lot more tax. At the 36000-yuan point, 1 yuan more bonus means 2310.10 yuan more tax. Near a critical point, either reduce the bonus to the point or increase it well past the blind-spot range.

Can I change my choice afterwards?

Yes. When you file the annual reconciliation the following year, you can switch to the other method in the system; the system usually displays the tax payable under both methods, and you confirm with the lower one. It’s advisable to click through both at reconciliation time before submitting.

How long does the separate-taxation policy last?

Under the current announcement, the policy of taxing annual one-time bonuses separately continues until December 31, 2027. Checked for this guide on 2026-11-04; whether it continues after that follows the official documents of the time.
